18.1 · Sparse attention: not attending to everything

FlashAttention removed the memory wall but left the FLOPs quadratic (Chapter 14). At 128k+ tokens that quadratic finally dominates. The 2025–2026 answer is to make attention genuinely sparse — compute only the scores that matter.

DeepSeek Sparse Attention (DSA)

Shipped in DeepSeek-V3.2. The mechanism has two stages:

  1. Lightning indexer. A very cheap scoring network — few heads, low-rank projections, FP8 arithmetic — scores every previous token against the current query using a ReLU-gated dot product. It is roughly an order of magnitude cheaper per FLOP than the main MLA attention.
  2. Top-k selection, then real attention. Take the k highest-scoring positions and run full MLA attention over only those.
O(L²)  →  O(L·k)     with k ≪ L the indexer still scans all L, but at a small constant; the expensive attention sees only k

DeepSeek reports substantial long-context training and inference speedups at "virtually identical" output quality. The related earlier work, NSA (Native Sparse Attention), trains sparsity in from scratch rather than retrofitting it, combining coarse token compression, selective fine-grained attention, and a sliding window.

Why this took so long

Sparse attention is an old idea (Longformer, BigBird, Reformer, 2020). Those used fixed patterns — strided, local, random — chosen in advance, and they consistently lost to dense attention on quality. The 2025–26 versions are learned and content-dependent: which tokens matter is decided per query at runtime. That difference, plus hardware-aware kernels that make irregular access patterns actually fast, is what finally made sparsity competitive.

18.2 · Hybrid architectures: attention + state space

The other attack on the quadratic is to replace most attention layers with something linear.

The state-space idea

An SSM maintains a fixed-size hidden state and updates it recurrently — like an RNN, but with a structured linear recurrence that can be computed in parallel during training via a scan.

AttentionSSM (Mamba)
Training costO(T²)O(T) via parallel scan
Inference per tokenO(T) — must read the whole KV cacheO(1) — fixed-size state
Memory during generationgrows linearly with Tconstant
Exact recall of arbitrary past tokensyesno — the state is a lossy summary

Mamba-2 (Dao & Gu, 2024) is the pivotal result: it showed SSMs and attention are two views of the same structured-matrix framework, differing in the structure of the underlying matrix. That connection let SSMs adopt attention's hardware-efficient training machinery, so Mamba-2 trains about as fast as a transformer while still running as an O(1) recurrence at inference.

Why hybrids, not pure SSMs

Pure SSMs underperform on tasks requiring exact retrieval — copying a phone number from 10k tokens back, or in-context learning that depends on precise induction. A fixed-size state cannot losslessly store an unbounded past. Attention can, because it keeps everything.

So production models interleave: mostly Mamba layers for cheap sequence mixing, with a minority of full attention layers for exact recall. Ratios of roughly 1 attention layer per 4–8 SSM layers are typical.

ModelPattern

The honest 2026 summary

Hybrids are clearly better than pure transformers at very long context (256k+) on cost and latency, and clearly not worse at short context. But the largest and strongest models as of mid-2026 — DeepSeek-V3.x, Qwen3, Kimi K2.x, and the closed frontier models — remain attention-based, typically with MoE and increasingly with sparse attention. Hybrids are winning the efficiency argument at long context; they have not yet won the frontier.

18.3 · Other things that are actually shipping

Multi-token prediction (MTP)

Train the model to predict the next n tokens, not just one, using extra lightweight heads. Gives a denser training signal, and at inference the extra heads act as a built-in draft model for speculative decoding — DeepSeek-V3 reports ~1.8× decoding speedup from its MTP module.

Test-time compute

The largest capability shift since instruction tuning. Instead of scaling training, spend more compute at inference: generate long chains of reasoning, sample many candidates, verify, and select. o1-style and R1-style reasoning models are trained with RL to use this budget well (Chapter 21). The scaling curve here — accuracy versus tokens-of-thinking — is a genuinely new axis, and it is currently steeper than the pretraining curve on reasoning benchmarks.

FP8 / FP4 training

DeepSeek-V3 was trained largely in FP8 with per-block scaling — a major cost reduction and one of the reasons its reported training cost was so low. Blackwell adds hardware support for even narrower formats. The engineering difficulty is entirely in the scaling strategy: which tensors need higher precision, and at what granularity.

Very large vocabularies and better tokenizers

The move to 128k–256k vocabularies (Chapter 3) continues, driven by long-context economics and multilingual coverage.

18.4 · What has not changed since 2017

Worth stating plainly, because the pace of announcements obscures it:

  • Residual stream + attention + MLP, stacked. The skeleton is identical.
  • Softmax attention over query–key dot products. Every proposed replacement — linear attention, kernel methods, pure SSMs — has so far ended up supplementing it rather than replacing it.
  • Next-token prediction as the pretraining objective. Unchanged.
  • Adam-family optimisation with warmup and decay. Muon is the first credible challenger in nine years.
  • Scaling works. Bigger, longer, more data still helps, with no clean ceiling yet observed.
How to read a new architecture paper

Ask four questions, in order: (1) Which cost does it reduce — parameters, FLOPs, memory, or bandwidth? (2) What does it give up to get that? (3) Was it validated at scale, or only at 100M parameters? (4) Is there a working kernel? An enormous number of published efficiency wins evaporate at scale or die because nobody wrote the CUDA. Chapters 12–16 give you everything you need to answer (1) and (2) from the abstract alone.

How does DSA reduce attention from O(L²) to O(L·k)?
It compresses the KV cache into a latent vector
A cheap indexer scores all positions, then expensive attention runs only over the top-k
It uses a fixed sliding window
It removes attention from most layers
Index cheaply, attend selectively. Option A is MLA (Ch 13) — DeepSeek-V3.2 uses both, and they're complementary: MLA shrinks what you store, DSA shrinks what you look at. Option C is the older fixed-pattern approach that lost on quality; option D describes hybrids.
Why do production models interleave Mamba with attention rather than going attention-free?
Mamba is slower to train
Attention layers are needed for positional encoding
A fixed-size state can't losslessly retain the past, so pure SSMs are weak at exact recall and induction; a few attention layers restore it
Mamba doesn't support GPUs well
Exact recall. This is the fundamental trade: O(1) inference state means lossy compression of history. Attention's KV cache is expensive precisely because it's lossless. Hybrids buy most of the efficiency while keeping enough attention layers to look things up exactly when needed.

18.5 · Exercises

18.1Implement top-k sparse attentioncode

Add a DSA-style path to your attention: compute full scores, keep only the top-k per query, mask the rest to −∞, and softmax. Then, on your Chapter 10 model, measure perplexity as a function of k ∈ {8, 16, 32, 64, all} at T=128.

  1. At what k does perplexity become indistinguishable from dense?
  2. What fraction of the sequence is that?
  3. Why is this a simulation rather than a real speedup?
Discussion

You'll typically find quality saturates well before k = T — attention distributions are genuinely sparse, with most mass on a small number of positions. That empirical fact is the entire premise of sparse attention.

It's a simulation because you still computed all the scores before discarding them. A real implementation needs a cheap way to predict the top-k without computing the full score matrix — which is exactly what the lightning indexer is, and why it must be much cheaper than the attention it replaces.

18.2Attention vs SSM on a recall taskthinking

Design a synthetic task that isolates the difference: a sequence of random key–value pairs followed by a query for one of the keys (the "associative recall" task from the Mamba literature). Train a 2-layer transformer and a 2-layer SSM (or use published results) at sequence lengths 64, 256, 1024.

What the literature shows

The transformer solves it at all lengths — it can attend directly to the matching key. Pure SSMs degrade as the number of pairs exceeds what the fixed state can hold. Adding even one attention layer to the SSM largely restores performance. This experiment is the cleanest justification for hybrid architectures, and it's small enough to actually run.

18.3Read a frontier paper properlyapplied

Pick one: the DeepSeek-V3 technical report, the Mamba-2 paper, or a current model's technical report. Write a one-page summary answering the four questions from §18.3's box, plus: what would you need to believe for this to not be adopted widely?

If you've worked through Chapters 1–17, the DeepSeek-V3 report should now be substantially readable — you know MLA, MoE, load balancing, FP8, MTP, and the KV-cache economics that motivate all of them. That's a good measure of how far you've come.
